Freeport road mound

Where does the ground under a highway come from? Three answers on one stretch of country, judged against the cell the mesher is actually drawing at. A road only ever rises (road::smooth takes the max both ways), so its profile is never under the ground and a mound is the only shape it ever needs: there is nothing to cut.

What this found, which is why it was drawn before it was built:
  • modes 1 and 2 are the same number at every cell. Measured: -0.14, -0.05, +0.08, +0.28, +0.08 m of terrain over the tarmac at 0.5, 2, 8, 16 and 32 m cells, identically. A mound whose vertices are the LEVELLED field is swallowed by exactly the thing that swallows the levelling, so it buys nothing at all against this defect
  • mode 3 holds at every cell, worst -1.43 m at 32 m cells, and the ground under it is never levelled: no corridor, no sites, untouched country
  • but the mound alone is not enough. At no minimum fill mode 3 is still swallowed at 16 and 32 m cells (+0.20 and +0.57 m). The fill is what makes it hold, and it is CHEAP: 0.25 m at a 16 m cell, 0.75 m at 32 m, against a raw chord error of 1.74 m and 4.50 m, because the embankment already stands proud where the chord is worst
  • walk off the verge and down the batter: the readout says whether your feet are on the road's own mound or on the terrain. A 1:3 batter is walkable and 1:2 is not, which is the number docs/civil-engineering.md gives
