Freeport marching cubes

A planet with a sea, sandy shores and green land as a density field, marched on the classic tables the core carries. The towns are in the same field: a building is a list of brushes (boxes, cylinders, spheres and ramps, added or cut, in concrete or plate) composed into it and dual contoured on a lattice six times finer under the town (one vertex a cell, where the crossings' planes meet, so a corner is a corner and a wall is a plane at any lattice, where marching cubes bevels every edge), so the ground, the walls, the floors and the ramps are one mesh with one collision rule. A material switches hard: a triangle is the deepest solid under it, and a plinth poured into the rock still meets it on a line. Press B on foot to place and cut blocks.

What to judge on foot, against the hex mockup on the same seed:
  • a slope is a slope: you walk up a hill and down to the beach without a step anywhere, and a cliff stops you
  • right angles are right angles and a wall is a plane, at 4x, 6x and 8x alike; the plinth melts into the ground and the concrete still stops on a line; the dome, the vault and the dugout are shapes a block kit cannot make
  • walk through a door, up the ramp, floor by floor, under the lamps, and look out of a window, which is an opening onto the room and nothing in it
  • B, then place a slab with the mouse and walk onto it, cut a hole in a wall and walk through: the picture is the collider
