Freeport marching cubes

A planet with a sea, sandy shores and green land as a density field, marched on the classic tables the core carries. The towns are in the same field: a building is a list of brushes (boxes, cylinders, spheres and ramps, added or cut, in concrete or plate) composed into it and dual contoured on a lattice six times finer under the town (a vertex a surface in each cell, where its crossings' planes meet, so a corner is a corner, a wall is a plane and a step is a step at any lattice, where marching cubes bevels every edge), so the ground, the walls, the floors and the ramps are one mesh with one collision rule. A material switches hard: a triangle is the deepest solid under it, and a plinth poured into the rock still meets it on a line. Press B on foot to build: pads of flat ground, walls, rooms, doors, blocks and cuts, in the same field.

What to judge on foot, against the hex mockup on the same seed:
  • a slope is a slope: you walk up a hill and down to the beach without a step anywhere, and a cliff stops you
  • right angles are right angles and a wall is a plane, at 4x, 6x and 8x alike; the plinth melts into the ground and the concrete still stops on a line; the dome, the vault and the dugout are shapes a block kit cannot make
  • walk through a door, up the ramp, floor by floor, under the lamps, and look out of a window, which is an opening onto the room and nothing in it
  • B, then build: a pad of flat ground where the crosshair is, walls from one click to the next, a room cut behind them, a door through one and a lamp under the slab, and walk in; cut a hole in a house and walk through: the picture is the collider, and nothing built is a special case of anything
