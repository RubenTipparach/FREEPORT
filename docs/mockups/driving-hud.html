Mockup 5 of the driving game

What a driver reads: the HUD and the map

The owner's ask, in one line: a proper gauge for the fuel, a speedometer, the money in the wallet, a frame counter in the corner, and a map to know where you are driving with markers you can put down by hand for a route. This page is that, drawn over a driving scene so the numbers move: the car below is on the road out of the port, burning its tank at the game's own eighty kilometres a fill, and every readout is read off it. Press M (or the button) for the map, click to drop a marker, and the compass strip at the top turns to the first one. G is not on the legend: it is the game's contextual key, and the HUD shows it as a PROMPT only while the car is stopped within reach of a pump (fill the tank, $10); on foot the same key buys a jerrycan at the kiosk or pours one into the car you own. Once the gauge is amber the car pulls in at the next pump it passes and waits, which is where the prompt can be seen, and S brakes anywhere. The clock in the corner is the game's own hour, over the frame counter; the hour MENU behind H is a debug panel and not a driver's control, so it is off the legend too.

A marker is a place and never a road: the legs are straight and the total is the crow's distance, which understates a drive round a bay. A route that FOLLOWS the roads between markers is A* over the road graph, and that is the next feature, not this one: the markers are its input, and this page is what the input looks like.

What this decides

The HUD is three glass panels and a strip, in the game's own type, with the money in the left corner and the two ROUND dials at the right, which is the owner's own placement. Nothing on it is a control except through a key, and every figure on it is one the core already computes: the mockup invents no number, it only lays them out. The map is a mode and not a screen, which is the RTS's sensors manager rule from swarm-demo: it dims the drive rather than leaving it, so a marker is set and the drive carries on behind it.

What it does not decide: the minimap. A small always-on map in a corner was drawn and taken out, because at the scale a car covers ground a corner map is either a blur of the road under the car or too coarse to place a pump on, and the compass strip carries the one thing a driver needs from it, which is where the next marker is.
