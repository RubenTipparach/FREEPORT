What a city block is

FREEPORT calls a 10 m square with one house on it a block, and lays a full two lane street with pavements both sides around every one of them. This page is what a block is on real ground, drawn to one scale, and what the arithmetic says it costs to get it wrong.

What the numbers say

A grid has one number that says whether it is a city or a car park: how much of the ground is street. It is 1 - (block / pitch)², and the pitch is the block plus the street, which is a distinction this codebase already makes (town::PITCH = BLOCK + STREET). Portland's famous 260 ft grid is a 200 ft block inside a 60 ft right of way: the 260 is the pitch.

Three things real blocks do that this one does not

A block holds many lots

A block is the ground inside four streets. Manhattan's is 80 by 274 m, Chicago's and Melbourne's 100 by 200, Barcelona's 113 square, Portland's 61 square. FREEPORT's is 10 m, which is one lot, so every house in the world stands on its own island with tarmac on four sides.

Lots front the street, two tiers deep

Subdivision codes put it plainly: a block is sized to hold two tiers of lots of approximately equal depth, back to back, with the rear boundaries or an alley between them. Lots are deep and narrow, 7.6 to 15 m of frontage by 30 to 38 m deep. Nobody plats a lot with no street on it.

The middle is not building

Cerdà drew the Eixample with two or three sides built around a garden. Developers filled all four sides and the interior too, which is the difference between the plan and the Barcelona that exists. Either way the interior is a decision, and a 4×4 block has one to make: four lots in the middle with no frontage.

What this suggests for FREEPORT

Keep the 10 m square as the lot, which is what the baked building library is cut to, and make a block four lots a side. The street grid then runs on block boundaries only. One rule decides the interior, and it is the zone rule already in the code:

Downtown — filled, 2×2 large

Four large buildings of 2×2 lots each fill the block. Every one of them is a corner building fronting two streets. This is the owner's own arithmetic and it is a Manhattan tower block.

Town — hollow, 12 round a court

The twelve perimeter lots build, the 2×2 interior is a courtyard. Every building fronts a street; nothing is platted without frontage. This is Cerdà's hollow block.

Suburb — hollow and sparse

The same perimeter, thinned by the fractal grain the towns already carry, with the interior as gardens. What makes a suburb a suburb stays the space, not the house.

What it would cost, named rather than hidden

Large buildings need a re-bake

The library is thirteen Blender bakes at width 10.0, depth 10.0. A 2×2 building is 20 m, so the large kinds want a bake at the new footprint. The guard for it already exists: Bake::models refuses a bake that does not fit the block its kind covers.

More buildings per town

Filling in street that should never have been street means more of everything: at 4×4 hollow blocks a town of the same radius carries about 1.7× the buildings. That is the point of the change and it is also its cost, in triangles, collision boxes and lamps.

Every street number moves

PITCH is read by the street mesh, the traffic graph, the route a stolen car takes out of town, and the slip a highway joins on. All of them derive it from one constant, so they follow — but every measured number in the design notes is re-measured.
